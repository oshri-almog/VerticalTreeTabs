Settings are saved as you change them, and every window uses them at once.

Tab groups

Your trees can also show in the browser's tab strip as tab groups: coloured, named after a tab's page, and collapsed when you collapse the tree in the panel.

Group the tabs of a tree in the tab strip

A group that starts below a tree's top tab has a colour of its own, in the panel too. Point at a choice to see it on the example.

Tab groups you make yourself

Groups you make in the tab strip (right-click a tab → Add tab to group), or from the panel.

Rename a group, or change its colour, from the right-click menu of any of its tabs in the panel, or by right-clicking the group's name in the tab strip.

New tabs

Where a new tab goes in the tree. “The current tab” is the one you were on when you opened it.

A New Tab page (Ctrl+T, or + in the tab strip)

No page opened it, so you choose where it goes.

A tab opened from a link

It goes under the page it was opened from (unless that page is a pinned tab). Choose where among that page's tabs.

A tab opened from a bookmark

A new tab for a bookmark: one you middle-click or Ctrl+click, say, or each of “Open all”.

Switch to a bookmark opened in the background

A bookmark you middle-click or Ctrl+click opens in the background.

The New tab button in the panel

The button under the tree. A double-click or middle-click on the empty space below the tree does the same.

Hold a key as you click New tab, or as you double-click or middle-click the space below the tree, to open the tab by the current tab: Shift first under it, Ctrl last under it, Alt next to it. The keyboard shortcut Alt+A opens a tab next to the current tab from anywhere.

Closing tabs

What happens to the tree when tabs close.

When a tab with tabs under it closes
When the current tab closes, switch to
Ask before closing several tabs at once

In the panel: Close tree, Close children, Close group, closing the tabs you selected, and Ctrl+click on a tab's ×, which closes it with the tabs under it. The keyboard shortcut that closes a tree does not ask.

Reopen a tab you closed with Ctrl+Shift+T or from History, and it goes back under the tab it was under, for half an hour after it closed.

Mouse & dragging

Clicking and dragging in the panel, and moving tabs in the browser's tab strip.

A tab dropped onto the middle of another tab

It goes under that tab, with the tabs under it.

Collapse or expand a tree with
A tab you move in the browser's tab strip

Whatever you choose here, a tab you drag out of its tree's tab group leaves the tree (the tabs under it move up), and one you drag into another tree's group joins that tree.

Drop on the top quarter of a tab to put the dragged tab before it, on the bottom quarter to put it after it. Hold Ctrl as you start dragging to move a tab without the tabs under it.
